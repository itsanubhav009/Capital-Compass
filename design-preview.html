    Smart Money Report / Capital Flow – India

    Foreign funds kept adding to large-cap financials through August

    A fourth straight month of net foreign buying, while domestic funds trimmed at the margin. The gap between the two is the story.

    23 Aug 20266 min readSAMPLEBANK · NSE
    Featured image
    In short

    Foreign institutional investors were net buyers for a fourth consecutive month. Domestic institutions were mild net sellers over the same window. Promoter holding was unchanged.

    The pattern that has held since May continued through August: foreign institutional investors added to large-cap financials while domestic funds took the other side. The net figures are modest in isolation. It is the persistence that is worth noting.

    What the filings show

    Shareholding data for the quarter puts foreign ownership at its highest level in seven quarters. Domestic institutional holding fell over the same period, though the move was small enough that a single large redemption could explain most of it.

    Promoter holding was unchanged, with no pledge activity disclosed. That absence is itself informative — in the two previous episodes of sustained foreign buying, promoter dilution followed within two quarters.

    What would change the read

    A reversal in the dollar, or a shift in the rate outlook, would be the obvious triggers. So would any change in promoter holding, which has been the most reliable early signal in this name historically.
